Smart Medication Support

I’m exploring how AI can help older adults keep track of medication. The prototype shows what it has recognised, explains what it plans to do, and gives the person a chance to change it.

Context
UTS doctoral research
Role
Research and prototyping
Users
Older adults
Focus
Keeping people in control
Challenge System Evidence

01 / Challenge

A reminder should be easy to understand and change.

A camera might recognise a medicine, and an app might remind someone to take it. But what happens when the camera gets it wrong, or the person’s routine changes? They need to see what happened and be able to put it right.

In my doctoral research, I’m looking at those moments: when the system should offer help, when it should ask first, and when it should leave the decision to the person.

02 / Prototype system

What happens after the camera sees a medicine?

01

Recognise

The camera helps identify a medicine and capture its details.

02

Explain

The screen shows the medicine, how sure the system is, and what it plans to do next.

03

Confirm

The person can accept, change, or decline a suggestion before it is carried out.

04

Remind and guide

Prompts on the screen and through sound make reminders clear and show what is happening.

05

Correct and recover

The person can review the record and correct a mistake or a change in their routine.

03 / What I’m looking at

Can people understand it and put mistakes right?

A

Show what is happening

People need to see what the camera recognised and why the system made a suggestion.

B

Let people choose how much help they want

Someone might welcome a reminder but want to check a medicine themselves. Those choices can change over time.

C

Make mistakes easy to correct

If the system gets something wrong, the next step should be easy to find.

D

Ask before making an important change

Ask the person to confirm changes that could affect their health or medication record.

E

Respect privacy

Explain what information is collected and let the person choose what to share.

F

Leave room for changes in daily life

Travel, visits from carers, and changes in routine all affect the kind of help someone needs.

04 / Research

Finding the right moment to ask.

I use the prototype to explore how different amounts of help affect people’s trust and sense of control. Small choices matter, such as whether a reminder is set automatically or shown for the person to confirm.
